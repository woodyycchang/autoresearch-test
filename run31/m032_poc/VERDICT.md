# M032 "Haggling Reward Model" — Proof-of-Concept Verdict

**Verdict: CONFIRMED at toy scale (evidence, not proof).**

Generated by `m032_poc.py` (pure numpy/scipy, matplotlib Agg). All numbers below
are the ACTUAL measured outputs of the run recorded in `results.json`
(S=4 skills, d=5, T=250 rounds, 12 seeds). Reproducible: fixed master seed
20260604; the script reruns end-to-end in ~15 s on CPU.

---

## The claim under test

In an RLHF-style loop, a reward model implemented as a per-skill Kalman belief
`(mu_s, Sigma_s)` that **actively probes the most-uncertain skill** and
**concedes by Kalman gain** will:

- **(a)** keep the proxy-vs-true reward gap smaller than a **frozen** RM, and
- **(b)** have its per-skill covariance `Sigma_s` **collapse while proxy reward
  is still rising**, and that collapse **precedes** the frozen RM's proxy-true
  gap **blow-up** — i.e. `Sigma_s`-collapse is a **leading indicator** of reward
  hacking / over-optimization.

## Decisive numbers (12 seeds, mean ± std)

| quantity | frozen RM | haggling RM |
|---|---|---|
| **FINAL proxy-true gap** | **+0.680 ± 0.023** | **+0.000 ± 0.054** |
| gap reduction (frozen − haggling) | — | **+0.680** |

- **Frozen reward-hacks**: gap blow-up on **12/12** seeds, AND true-quality
  **peaks-then-declines** on **9/12** seeds (the hallmark of over-optimization:
  RM score keeps climbing to ~1.45 while *true* quality peaks ~0.80 then falls).
- **Sigma collapses** (max-over-skills `Sigma` < 10% of its initial value) on
  **12/12** seeds.
- **LEAD TIME** (`frozen_blowup_step − sigma_collapse_step`):
  **+5.08 ± 3.07 rounds**.
- **Fraction of seeds with positive lead: 1.00 (12/12).** The Sigma-collapse
  precedes the frozen blow-up on every seed.

### Robustness (recorded in `results.json → robustness_sweep_over_exploit_weight`)

The finding is **not a single tuned point**. Sweeping the RM's spurious exploit
weight from 0.30 → 0.90:

| exploit w | frozen final gap | haggling final gap | blow-ups | lead (mean±std) | pos-lead frac |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.30 | +0.405 | −0.002 | 12/12 | +10.42 ± 2.84 | 1.00 |
| 0.45 | +0.557 | +0.003 | 12/12 | +5.83 ± 4.24 | 0.83 |
| 0.55 | +0.680 | +0.000 | 12/12 | +5.08 ± 3.07 | 1.00 |
| 0.70 | +0.888 | +0.002 | 12/12 | +3.92 ± 2.81 | 0.83 |
| 0.90 | +1.195 | +0.003 | 12/12 | +3.42 ± 2.29 | 0.83 |

Across the whole range: haggling keeps the gap ≈ 0; frozen always reward-hacks;
lead time is always **positive** (collapse leads). As the exploit gets stronger
the hack develops faster, so the lead shrinks monotonically — a sensible,
mechanistic trend, not an artifact.

## Threshold definitions used (declared up front, in `results.json`)

- **Sigma-collapse step** = first round where `max_s Sigma_s` < `0.10 ×
  max_s Sigma_s(init)`. `Sigma` is **drift-coupled** (re-inflated by a saturating
  process-noise term proportional to how far the policy's response drifted since
  its last probe), so this is a genuine first-crossing of a coupled stochastic
  process — *not* a fixed Kalman countdown.
- **Frozen blow-up step** = first round (≥ 3) where the frozen proxy-true gap
  exceeds `(2+1) × |baseline|`, with `baseline` = mean gap over rounds [0,3)
  (i.e. before any hacking has developed).
- **Frozen counted as genuinely hacking** only if it shows BOTH a gap blow-up
  AND a true-quality peak-then-decline (peak before round T−5, decline > 0.02)
  on a majority of seeds. This gate is what kept earlier design iterations
  honestly **INCONCLUSIVE** until the toy produced *real* over-optimization.
- **Lead time** = `frozen_blowup_step − sigma_collapse_step` (positive ⇒ leads).

## Why this toy is decisive (and how it avoids rigging)

The mechanism that creates over-optimization is real, not hand-set:
- True quality is **bounded/saturating** (`sum_j a_j tanh(b_j x_j)`) in the
  legitimate dims and **exactly zero** along an exploit dim; the linear RM
  carries a spurious **positive** weight on that exploit dim.
- The policy hill-climbs under a **capacity budget** (`||x|| ≤ 2.5`, projected
  each step). Because budget is finite, magnitude spent pumping the exploit dim
  is **stolen** from legitimate dims → true quality peaks then **declines**.
- Early rounds: legitimate gradients dominate → proxy and true both rise
  (productive, in-distribution learning). The haggling belief collapses here.
- Later: legit dims saturate, the (deliberately weak) exploit gradient takes
  over → proxy keeps climbing, true declines → the gap **blows up**, *after* the
  belief has already collapsed.

The earlier, simpler design versions were honestly logged as **INCONCLUSIVE**:
the first had a deterministic Kalman countdown (zero-variance "lead"), the second
had a monotone-linear gap (no real blow-up), the third saturated true quality
without any decline (no over-optimization). Only after introducing the capacity
budget did the toy exhibit genuine reward-hacking, at which point the haggling
RM's advantage and the leading-indicator relationship appeared — and survived a
parameter sweep.

## Honest caveats — this is a TOY (confirming ⇒ evidence, NOT proof)

1. **The haggling RM is handed an oracle.** Its measurement is a noisy reading of
   *true quality itself* (the "revealed reservation value"). Real RLHF has no
   such oracle — it has human/AI preference labels that are themselves gameable.
   A real test must use a noisy, *biasable* elicitation channel, not ground truth.
2. **The re-anchor is a soft additive correction** in a 1-D belief space per
   skill. Real RMs are high-dimensional neural nets; "re-anchoring scoring toward
   `mu_s`" would be a non-trivial fine-tuning/regularization operation with its
   own optimization dynamics and failure modes.
3. **"Skills" are independent scalars.** Real skills share representation;
   probing one perturbs others. Cross-skill interference could erase the clean
   per-skill `argmax Sigma` active-probing signal.
4. **The collapse step has little variance here (≈ round 3).** With 4 skills and
   one probe/round, active probing drives `Sigma` below threshold fast and almost
   deterministically; the lead-time *variance* comes almost entirely from
   variance in the *blow-up* timing. So the toy shows "collapse is early and
   precedes blow-up" robustly, but does **not** establish that collapse *timing*
   finely tracks *which* run will hack worse.
5. **Linear frozen RM vs concave truth** is the cleanest possible
   over-optimization setup. Real RM extrapolation error is messier and may not
   blow up so reliably (12/12 here is partly a consequence of the clean toy).
6. **No KL/early-stopping baseline.** The practical question is whether
   `Sigma`-collapse beats the standard mitigations (KL-to-reference penalty,
   reward-model ensembles/uncertainty, early stopping). This toy does not compare
   against them.

## What a full GPU-scale test would settle that this toy cannot

- **Real RLHF**: an actual LM policy + a learned neural RM + PPO/GRPO, with the
  haggling belief layered over the RM's *latent* skill estimates rather than a
  scalar oracle. Does `Sigma`-collapse still precede measured reward-hacking when
  the measurement channel is preference labels, not ground truth?
- **Predictive power, quantified**: treat `Sigma`-collapse as a binary/continuous
  *predictor* of subsequent gap blow-up across many runs and report ROC-AUC /
  precision-at-fixed-recall and the *distribution* of lead times — not just a
  mean sign. The toy's near-constant collapse step prevents this.
- **Head-to-head vs standard mitigations**: does active-probing + Kalman concede
  reduce true-quality regret more than (a) KL penalty, (b) RM ensemble
  disagreement, (c) early stopping on a held-out gold RM — at equal compute?
- **Cross-skill / representation sharing**: with entangled skills, does
  `argmax Sigma` active probing still find the hack-prone skill, or does
  interference destroy the per-skill signal?
- **Scaling**: does the lead time (in optimization steps or KL budget) grow,
  shrink, or vanish as model/RM capacity and the strength of the exploit channel
  scale — i.e. is the early-warning window usable in practice or does it close as
  models get more capable at finding exploits?
